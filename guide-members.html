Signing up

  • Join: press Join on the roster in the event’s thread. What clicking Interested on the Discord event does depends on the server: it signs you up too, puts you on standby until you press Join, or just follows the event (you’re told if its time changes). The bot can’t mark you Interested, so after Join it links to the event in case you want Discord’s own reminder. Your place in line is the time the bot first saw you sign up; if Interested only put you on standby or following, your place counts from pressing Join.
  • Leave: remove Interested, or press Leave. If you come back within the server’s grace period (5 minutes unless changed), you keep your original place. While your place is held, Leave offers Withdraw now to give it up straight away, also after removing Interested.
  • Your role: by default you’re matched on the roles you already have. Change role lets you pick a specific role you’re bringing, “Any role I have”, or Standby.
  • Preferences: in /whoami you can say which roles you prefer, and which you’d rather not play. The bot uses them when placing you on rosters, but they never cost you your place, and choosing a role for one event always wins. Filling the event's named role slots comes first: if you're the only one who can fill one, you're placed there even if you prefer another role. A flex slot (shown as “RDM (flex)”) takes its role first; anyone can stand in until someone with the role signs up. If that happens before the pre-event roster, the stand-in is told once, stays signed up and is first in line for the next place.
  • Role fit: the last step of /whoami is for a hard no: pick a preset (such as Odyssey) and the roles you’re never to be placed as in its events. You keep the role and its pings; choosing a role for one event, or an organizer placing you, still wins.
  • Standby: you wait outside the roster and are moved into an open slot shortly before the event (an hour by default), or earlier if an organizer fills from standby. You get a mention when that happens.

The roster

  • The roster appears in the event’s thread with the first signup and updates as people join and leave. Earlier signups are never bumped by later ones. Open slots are listed with the role each needs.
  • If an event needs particular roles, players are placed into slots that fit them; anyone who can’t fit is listed under Invalid signups with the reason.
  • If someone withdraws from a full roster, the next eligible player is promoted and a short notice says who replaced whom.
  • Players are shown as a mention with their server name after it. Edits to the roster never notify anyone; shortly before the start (15 minutes by default) the bot posts a fresh Pre-event roster, which does notify the players placed on it.
  • If the start time changes, everyone signed up is mentioned with the old and new times.
  • When the event ends, its thread is archived: it disappears from the channel list but its history stays.

Your static

A static’s card shows its members and a welcome explaining who to contact and how to plan its events. Look for it in the channel’s pinned messages; if someone unpins it, the bot leaves that choice alone. Leaders use Manage, then choose Welcome message… to write their own welcome. Leaving it blank restores the default, and mentions in it never ping.

Statics are set up by admins. Ask an admin to create one, rename it or dissolve it. Existing statics keep working. If you lead one, open Manage on its card or /static. The dropdown offers Members to add or remove people and Welcome message… to edit the welcome. On Paid, an admin can create private text and voice channels with a new static. See Statics for the details.

Static events normally appear in the static’s text channel, or the channel holding its card. If someone opens enrollment for a date, follow the old thread’s link to its new public thread in the announcement channel. The next date returns to the static’s channel and is restricted again.

/report-bug scope [screenshots]anywhere, during community testingReport a bug to the bot’s developers. Pick what it affects, attach up to three pictures, videos or text files, and fill in the form (what isn’t working, the steps, what should happen and what happens instead, and when). The report is posted, numbered like BUG-0042, in the bug channel of the EventMoogle community server, mentioning you if you’re a member there; the bot saves diagnostics with it for the developers (see the Privacy Policy). A few reports an hour at most.

Short private replies from the bot disappear after a couple of minutes. Private panels, such as /help, /signups and menus, stay until they expire, then say so; run the command again for a fresh one.
