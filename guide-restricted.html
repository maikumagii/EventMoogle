Restricted events and statics

Restricting who can sign up

Only members with certain roles can sign up: put Restricted: Static, Officers (role or category names) in a Discord event’s description, or set Who can sign up on a preset in /settings → Presets. The description line wins over the preset, and Restricted: no lets anyone sign up for that one event. Pinging a role (or a static) never restricts who can sign up.

Static presets: choose Static… on a preset to make it one static’s events, in place of a private channel. Its members are added to each roster and asked to confirm, only they can sign up, the static is pinged, and the thread is private (see below). Open enrollment opens one date to everyone without showing the private thread’s history. A preset has one static at most; Who can sign up applies only to presets without one.

  • Others who press Join get a private reply naming the roles; their Interested clicks don’t put them on the roster. Members who gain a role appear straight away.
  • Members already signed up when a restriction is added stay, marked 🔒 on the roster; remove them by hand if needed. Organizers can always add anyone.
  • Pings: a restricted event pings only the roles it’s restricted to (for a Static preset, the static), in place of the preset’s usual pings. A private event without a restriction pings nobody. Once enrollment is open on a public event, reminders ping as usual for that date.

Private threads

  • Restricted events get a private thread by default (/settings → Statics → Restricted events private turns that off). Private: no in the description keeps one event public, and Private: yes makes any event private. Either line counts only before the bot posts the event, since a thread can’t change type later.
  • The bot adds the restricted roles, the event’s creator, and everyone who signs up. It needs Create Private Threads in the channel; without it, the event shows an error rather than a public thread.

Open enrollment

  • The event’s thread has an Open enrollment button. Any member with one of the roles (or the organizer) can press it to let anyone sign up for this date; it then disappears.
  • On a private event, Discord can’t make the thread public, so the button asks first, then moves the event to a new public thread (where anyone can see the roster and press Join for that date) and closes the private one (locked, kept to read). The new thread is announced like a public event.
  • On a recurring event the next date is restricted (and private) again, and members without the role who were only let in for one date leave its roster.

Statics

A static is a fixed group, such as a raid static. EventMoogle stores its members in the database, so Restricted: Raid Static works even without a Discord role. With Role management enabled, new statics also get a role with no permissions. Existing linked-role members are imported from a complete Discord member cache; later manual Discord role changes stay in sync. Incomplete imports remain pending.

Turning Role management off preserves the group and its Discord role; membership controls still edit the group but stop changing Discord roles. Deleting a linked role preserves the group and its restrictions. Group pings use the role when its complete member list exactly matches the group and that saves pings, otherwise they mention members individually.

  • Admins manage statics in /settings → Statics: Create static… names it; add and remove members; Leader and card… sets its leader and where its card goes; Dissolve… removes the group and, with Role management enabled, takes its linked role from members or deletes it.
  • Leaders add and remove members by right-clicking a member → Apps → Add to my static or Remove from my static (asked which, if they lead several), or with Manage members on the static’s card.
  • The card: a post in a channel admins choose, usually the static’s own, showing its leader and members. It updates as members come and go.
  • The static button: Static button… posts a button in a channel you choose so any member can make statics of their own. Create a static asks for a name and what it’s for (one of the server’s ping roles, or none); each member can have one static per ping role, plus one without. They lead it, and My statics lets them add and remove members, rename it, change what it’s for, or dissolve it, for their own statics only.
