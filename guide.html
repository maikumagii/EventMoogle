Event Moogle User Guide

Event Moogle turns Discord scheduled events into signup lists and rosters. Members sign up by clicking Interested on an event or Join on its roster; the bot keeps everyone in the order they signed up, fills the roster’s slots, and keeps a thread for each event up to date.

For members

Signing up

  • Join: press Join on the roster in the event’s thread. What clicking Interested on the Discord event does depends on the server: it signs you up too, puts you on standby until you press Join, or just follows the event (you’re told if its time changes). The bot can’t mark you Interested, so after Join it links to the event in case you want Discord’s own reminder. Your place in line is the time the bot first saw you sign up; if Interested only put you on standby or following, your place counts from pressing Join.
  • Leave: remove Interested, or press Leave. If you come back within the server’s grace period (5 minutes unless changed), you keep your original place. After pressing Leave, Withdraw now gives your place up straight away instead.
  • Your role: by default you’re matched on the roles you already have. Change role lets you pick a specific role you’re bringing, “Any role I have”, or Standby.
  • Preferences: in /whoami you can say which roles you prefer, and which you’d rather not play. The bot uses them when placing you on rosters, but they never cost you your place, and choosing a role for one event always wins.
  • Standby: you wait outside the roster and are moved into an open slot shortly before the event (an hour by default), or earlier if an organizer fills from standby. You get a mention when that happens.

The roster

  • The roster appears in the event’s thread with the first signup and updates as people join and leave. Earlier signups are never bumped by later ones.
  • If an event needs particular roles, players are placed into slots that fit them; anyone who can’t fit is listed under Invalid signups with the reason.
  • If someone withdraws from a full roster, the next eligible player is promoted and a short notice says who replaced whom.
  • Shortly before the start (15 minutes by default) the bot posts a fresh Pre-event roster, which mentions the players placed on it. Until then the roster shows members by their server names, since edits to it don't notify anyone.
  • If the start time changes, everyone signed up is mentioned with the old and new times.
  • When the event ends, its thread is archived: it disappears from the channel list but its history stays.

For event organizers

The person who created a Discord event, and server administrators, can organize it. Organizer tools are in the roster’s Manage button (or /roster manage) and the /roster command, used inside the event’s thread.

Choosing the roster

  • Presets are rosters the server has saved, such as “Raid: 2 Tanks, 2 Healers, 4 DPS” or “Scrim: 5 players”. An event whose name contains a preset’s keyword gets that preset automatically; otherwise it gets the server’s default roster. Keywords match whole words and their plurals (raid matches “Raids”), and mage + sortie needs both words in any order (“Sortie with Mages”). Presets are in an order the admins set: the first one with a matching keyword wins. Until someone other than you signs up (Discord marks you Interested when you create the event), renaming the event picks the matching preset again.
  • After that, renaming the event to match a different preset posts an Apply / Keep current prompt for you.
  • /roster preset (or Manage → Apply a preset) applies any preset by hand.
  • /roster expand adds a party, copied from the preset’s repeatable party and adjusted in a form; /roster shrink removes the last party.
  • /roster slots and /roster role set this event’s own size and role slots.

Recurring events

Each date of a repeating Discord event gets its own post, thread and roster. When a date ends, its thread is archived and the next date is posted. Members who are Interested in the series carry over to every date, keeping their place in line; Not interested on one date leaves them out of that date only. Join counts for one date: mark Interested to stay on the series.

Confirm Attendance (on by default; /settings → Recurring events): members Interested in the series keep a reserved place on each date and confirm it. In Sign up mode they press Join (and Leave means “not this date”: they stay on the series); in Everything mode they press Confirm or Can't make it. In Standby and Follow only modes, pressing Join for each date already is the confirmation. The roster shows ✅ for confirmed and ⏳ for waiting. One reminder pings those who haven't answered (2 hours before the deadline by default). At the deadline (4 hours before the start by default), anyone who hasn't confirmed moves aside and standbys take their places; confirming later puts them back on standby. Joining, or marking Interested on that date, counts as confirming; Discord's Not interested on that date counts as Can't make it. On Paid, /attendance lookup shows how often each member confirmed, declined or didn't answer.

No-show rule (Paid, off by default; /settings → Attendance rules): members who missed more than a set share of their recent signups (no-shows in attendance, or not confirming a recurring date) wait on standby first, behind members who chose standby, and are called in at the standby fill time if places remain. Saying Can't make it in time isn't a miss. /attendance me shows where you stand; admins can exempt someone with /attendance exempt.

Voice-required events: put Voice: required in a Discord event’s description. When attendance is confirmed, members marked attended who weren’t seen in its voice channel get half attendance (🎧); it counts as half in the rate and half a miss for the no-show rule. Turn this off in /settings → Attendance rules; voice presence is always recorded, so switching it applies to past events too.

Restricted events

Only members with certain roles can sign up: put Restricted: Static, Officers (role or category names) in a Discord event’s description, or set Who can sign up on a preset in /settings → Presets. The description line wins over the preset.

  • Others who press Join get a private reply naming the roles; their Interested clicks don’t put them on the roster. Members who gain a role appear straight away.
  • Members already signed up when a restriction is added stay, marked 🔒 on the roster; remove them by hand if needed. You can always add anyone yourself.
  • Open enrollment: the thread has an Open enrollment button. Any member with one of the roles (or the organizer) can press it to let anyone sign up for this date; it then disappears. On a recurring event the next date is restricted again, and members without the role who were only let in for one date leave its roster.
  • Private threads: restricted events get a private thread by default (/settings → Statics → Restricted events private turns that off); Private: no in the description keeps one event public, and Private: yes makes any event private. Either line counts only before the bot posts the event. Discord can’t make a private thread public, so on a private event Open enrollment asks first, then moves the event to a new public thread (where anyone can see the roster and press Join for that date) and closes the private one (locked, kept to read). The new thread is announced like a public event. The bot adds the restricted roles, you, and everyone who signs up. Members outside the roles sign up with Discord’s Interested button once enrollment is open. The bot needs Create Private Threads in the channel.
  • Pings: a restricted event pings only its roles, never ping rules or the default notification. A private event without a restriction pings nobody. Once enrollment is open on a public event, reminders ping as usual for that date.
  • Statics: a fixed group with its own role (see /settings → Statics); Restricted: Raid Static restricts an event to it.

Setting up from the event description

Write a block like this in the Discord event’s description:

Roster: @Aldric, Brynna, Corvin
Open slots: 2
Slots: Healer, 2x Tank
  • Listed players (mentions, or names that match exactly one member) take the first slots and are signed up for you. In servers that sign up only through Discord’s Interested button, their places are reserved until they click Interested.
  • Open slots adds slots anyone can fill; Slots adds slots for roles by name (Open for an open one).
  • Edit the description and the roster follows, until someone who isn’t listed (other than you, the creator) signs up. Names or roles the bot can’t find are listed in the thread.

Arranging players

Press Manage on the roster (or run /roster manage in the event’s thread). The private panel does it all:

  • Choose a player from the list, then Move… (pin them into a party; Paid), Swap… (with another player; Paid: Swap parties moves each to the other’s party, into a slot they fit; Swap places gives each the other’s exact slot and its role, and keeps them there), Unpin, Remove (exclude them, even if Interested) or Set role… (the role they’re bringing).
  • Add a player puts someone at the front of the signups.
  • Slots… sets this event’s size and role slots; apply a preset; add or remove a party; Fill from standby now; Attendance… (Paid).

/roster has manage, attendance and export (a CSV of the roster; Paid). With Expanded commands on (/settings → General), it also has typed versions of every panel action: move, swap, unpin, add, remove, set-role, fill, slots, role, preset, expand and shrink. The panel lists the first 25 signups; for bigger events, use the typed commands.

Attendance (Paid)

  • For events in a voice channel, the bot notes who was in the channel during the event and pre-marks them as attended.
  • /roster attendance (or Manage → Attendance…) opens a checklist to confirm or correct it, during or after the event.
  • /attendance lookup and /attendance recent show history and attendance rates (for members who can manage events).
  • /attendance export downloads every attendance mark and recurring-date answer as a spreadsheet (CSV), or one member’s.

For server admins

When the bot is added, it posts a short welcome in the server’s system channel. Commands appear as they become useful: the event commands once there’s an announcement channel, /roles and /whoami once there’s something to pick. New to the bot? Run /setup (needs Manage Server): it walks through the basics step by step and disappears once you finish. /about-new-features (needs Manage Server, first in the command list) shows what’s new, page by page: the updates your admins haven’t read yet, otherwise the latest one. /settings has everything for later, including Run setup again.

Run /settings (needs Manage Server). It opens a private panel with sections; switch between them with the Section menu at the top:

Roles can be typed by name. If a name matches no role, the form offers to create it: new roles have no permissions and go to the bottom of the role list. Unused roles… (under Self-assignable roles) lists roles the bot created that nothing uses any more, split into those nobody has and those people have, with a delete button for each (the second asks twice). If several roles share a name, it offers to remove the extra copies (the oldest is kept, and only copies with no members and no permissions are removed). A role that already exists is never created again. The bot needs Manage Roles for that, and for category roles and /roles, with its own role above the roles it manages.

Free and Paid

Free is the complete bot: signups, rosters with parties, buttons, standby, reminders, presets (the first two in your order apply automatically by keyword, the rest by hand), description setup, restricted events, every message template, 10 ping rules, and 15 events maintained at a time.

Paid, a server subscription bought in Discord, adds automation and reporting: every preset applied by keyword, automatic category roles, moving and swapping players, attendance with history and stats, the audit log, CSV export, 25 ping rules, 50 events at a time, and priority when the bot is busy. If a subscription ends, nothing is deleted; Paid features pause until you subscribe again.

Privacy

  • /privacy redact hides your name and mentions in the bot’s posts, everywhere, while your signups keep working.
  • /privacy delete deletes your signups and attendance everywhere and stops the bot tracking you. Signing up again yourself turns tracking back on. /privacy resume undoes both redact and delete from then on: the bot tracks you and shows your name again (deleted data and posts already redacted stay as they are).
  • Data about ended events is deleted automatically after 90 days; attendance records are kept. Details are in the Privacy Policy.

Words used here

  • Slot: one place on a roster, either for a role (Tank, Healer) or open to anyone.
  • Party: a group of slots; a roster can have several, up to 25 slots in total.
  • Category: a name (with or without a role of its own) that covers several roles, such as Healer covering Cleric and Druid.
  • Preset: a saved roster the server can reuse, picked by keywords in event names.
  • Ping rule: keywords that make a new event mention certain roles.
  • Standby: a signup that waits outside the roster until shortly before the event.
