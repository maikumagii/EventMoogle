Event Moogle User Guide

Event Moogle turns Discord scheduled events into signup lists and rosters. Members sign up by clicking Interested on an event or Join on its roster; the bot keeps everyone in the order they signed up, fills the roster’s slots, and keeps a thread for each event up to date.

For members

Signing up

  • Join: press Join on the roster in the event’s thread. What clicking Interested on the Discord event does depends on the server: it signs you up too, puts you on standby until you press Join, or just follows the event (you’re told if its time changes). The bot can’t mark you Interested, so after Join it links to the event in case you want Discord’s own reminder. Your place in line is the time the bot first saw you sign up; if Interested only put you on standby or following, your place counts from pressing Join.
  • Leave: remove Interested, or press Leave. If you come back within the server’s grace period (15 minutes unless changed), you keep your original place.
  • Your role: by default you’re matched on the roles you already have. Change role lets you pick a specific role you’re bringing, “Any role I have”, or Standby.
  • Preferences: in /whoami you can say which roles you prefer, and which you’d rather not play. The bot uses them when placing you on rosters, but they never cost you your place, and choosing a role for one event always wins.
  • Standby: you wait outside the roster and are moved into an open slot shortly before the event (an hour by default), or earlier if an organizer fills from standby. You get a mention when that happens.

The roster

  • The roster appears in the event’s thread with the first signup and updates as people join and leave. Earlier signups are never bumped by later ones.
  • If an event needs particular roles, players are placed into slots that fit them; anyone who can’t fit is listed under Invalid signups with the reason.
  • If someone withdraws from a full roster, the next eligible player is promoted and a short notice says who replaced whom.
  • Shortly before the start (15 minutes by default) the bot posts a fresh Pre-event roster.
  • If the start time changes, everyone signed up is mentioned with the old and new times.
  • When the event ends, its thread is archived: it disappears from the channel list but its history stays.

For event organizers

The person who created a Discord event, and server administrators, can organize it. Organizer tools are in the roster’s Manage button (or /roster manage) and the /roster command, used inside the event’s thread.

Choosing the roster

  • Presets are rosters the server has saved, such as “Raid: 2 Tanks, 2 Healers, 4 DPS” or “Scrim: 5 players”. An event whose name contains a preset’s keyword gets that preset automatically; otherwise it gets the server’s default roster. Until someone other than you signs up (Discord marks you Interested when you create the event), renaming the event picks the matching preset again.
  • After that, renaming the event to match a different preset posts an Apply / Keep current prompt for you.
  • /roster preset (or Manage → Apply a preset) applies any preset by hand.
  • /roster expand adds a party, copied from the preset’s repeatable party and adjusted in a form; /roster shrink removes the last party.
  • /roster slots and /roster role set this event’s own size and role slots.

Setting up from the event description

Write a block like this in the Discord event’s description:

Roster: @Aldric, Brynna, Corvin
Open slots: 2
Slots: Healer, 2x Tank
  • Listed players (mentions, or names that match exactly one member) take the first slots and are signed up for you. In servers that sign up only through Discord’s Interested button, their places are reserved until they click Interested.
  • Open slots adds slots anyone can fill; Slots adds slots for roles by name (Open for an open one).
  • Edit the description and the roster follows, until someone who isn’t listed (other than you, the creator) signs up. Names or roles the bot can’t find are listed in the thread.

Attendance (Paid)

  • For events in a voice channel, the bot notes who was in the channel during the event and pre-marks them as attended.
  • /roster attendance (or Manage → Attendance…) opens a checklist to confirm or correct it, during or after the event.
  • /attendance member and /attendance recent show history and attendance rates (for members who can manage events).

For server admins

When the bot is added, it posts a short welcome in the server’s system channel. New to the bot? Run /setup (needs Manage Server): it walks through the basics step by step and disappears once you finish. /settings has everything for later, including Run setup again.

Run /settings (needs Manage Server). It opens a private panel with sections; switch between them with the Section menu at the top:

Free and Paid

Free is the complete bot: signups, rosters with parties, buttons, standby, reminders, presets (the two oldest apply automatically by keyword, the rest by hand), description setup, every message template, 10 ping rules, and 15 events maintained at a time.

Paid, a server subscription bought in Discord, adds automation and reporting: every preset applied by keyword, bot-managed category roles, moving and swapping players, attendance with history and stats, 25 ping rules, 50 events at a time, and priority when the bot is busy. If a subscription ends, nothing is deleted; Paid features pause until you subscribe again.

Privacy

  • /privacy redact hides your name and mentions in the bot’s posts, everywhere, while your signups keep working.
  • /privacy delete deletes your signups and attendance everywhere and stops the bot tracking you. Signing up again yourself, or /privacy resume, turns tracking back on.
  • Data about ended events is deleted automatically after 90 days; attendance records are kept. Details are in the Privacy Policy.

Words used here

  • Slot: one place on a roster, either for a role (Tank, Healer) or open to anyone.
  • Party: a group of slots; a roster can have several, up to 25 slots in total.
  • Category: a role that covers several others, such as Healer covering Cleric and Druid.
  • Preset: a saved roster the server can reuse, picked by keywords in event names.
  • Ping rule: keywords that make a new event mention certain roles.
  • Standby: a signup that waits outside the roster until shortly before the event.
