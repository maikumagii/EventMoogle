2. Information we process

The bot receives information through Discord’s API, event notifications, and slash commands. It can record people who mark Interested on a monitored event even if they never run a command. Authorized users can also add people to the bot’s signup records.

  • Identifiers: Discord user, server, event, role, channel, thread, and message IDs.
  • Signup and roster records: first-observed signup times, withdrawal and rejoin state, ordering, registered role IDs and names, role assignments, manual inclusion or exclusion, and, for recurring events, your Interested or Not interested answers for single dates and whether you confirmed, declined or didn't answer for each date (kept with attendance records).
  • Event and member information: event names, descriptions, start times, status, creator identity, member names, and server roles used to display events, check permissions, and match players to slots. Some information is fetched as needed; event details can also appear in stored generated messages.
  • Configuration and delivery records: server and event settings, which release notes a server’s admins last read and who read them, templates, role groups, reminder requests and cooldowns, generated roster and notification text, mentioned user IDs, and message delivery or cleanup state.
  • Member roles (Server Members intent): when the operator enables it, Discord sends member and role updates so rosters can match players to slots and the bot can keep category roles in step. These are kept in memory, not stored, apart from the role assignments above.
  • Attendance (servers on the Paid plan): for events held in a voice channel, which members were present in that channel during the event (sampled every few minutes), and attendance marks (attended or no-show) with each event’s name, start time and final roster. Each mark keeps when you were first seen in the event’s voice channel, so a late arrival can be told apart, and whether a server admin voided the event’s attendance (and who).
  • Audit log (servers on the Paid plan): while a server is on the Paid plan, a record of who did what and when: settings changes, hand roster edits, attendance actions, each signup, withdrawal, standby move and recurring-date answer, members joining and leaving the server (with the Server Members intent), and the roles, nicknames, threads and posts the bot changed, with the member who caused it. Server admins (Manage Server) can read and download it.
  • Safety records: members a server’s admins blocked from giving themselves roles or signing up for events, and which elevated roles admins confirmed (with who confirmed them). Blocks are the server’s safety records, so a deletion request removes an admin’s name from them but doesn’t lift a block on the person asking. Security alerts (for example, a member pressing role buttons too fast) are posted to the admin channel a server chooses, which its admins can read. The bot counts recent presses in memory only, to pause members who press too fast.
  • Restricted events: for events only some roles can join, the IDs of members kept on the roster because they signed up before the restriction applied, who opened enrollment for a date, and which members the bot added to a private event thread.
  • Statics: each static’s name, its role, its leader, and who created it. Membership is holding the role, which Discord stores.
  • Subscriptions: Discord’s records of a server’s subscription to the bot (entitlement and product IDs and start and end times). Payments are handled by Discord; the bot never receives payment details.
  • Profile choices: the roles you say you prefer or would rather not play, the roles you’re never to be placed as in a given preset’s events (/whoami), and whether you have completed /whoami. Nickname and role changes are made in Discord and not stored by the bot.
  • Privacy lists: the Discord IDs of people who asked for redaction or deletion, so those requests keep being honored.
  • Operational logs: connection and error information, signup actions, event and server IDs, and, for some actions, affected user IDs, role IDs, and the ID of the person making the change.

The bot reads relevant channel and thread history to recover its own previously sent messages and avoid duplicate posts. It does not maintain a general archive of members’ conversations. The bot does not request your Discord password, email address, payment details, or private direct-message history.

3. How information is used

Information is used to maintain signup order, apply role and capacity rules, manage withdrawal grace periods, display rosters, send reminders and schedule-change notices, record and summarize attendance where a server uses it, decide which features a server’s plan includes, enforce command permissions and cooldowns, recover after restarts, and troubleshoot failures. Attendance history may be used in the future to inform signup priority; this policy will be updated before that happens. The bot does not use this information for advertising, sell it, or use it to train AI models.

4. Who can see information

Signup names, mentions, selected roles, roster eligibility, and changes may appear in event threads and command responses. People who can access those Discord channels may see public messages. Some command responses are private to the invoking user, but underlying signup records are not necessarily private. Administrators and designated bot operators can manage records through authorized commands.

The operator and hosting providers involved in running the service may access stored records and logs as needed to operate and support it. Data is sent to Discord to provide the bot’s features. Information may also be disclosed where required by law. Discord processes information under its own Privacy Policy.

5. Storage and retention

The bot stores records in a SQLite database on its host. In Docker deployments, the database normally lives in a persistent volume and survives container replacement and upgrades. Logs are written to the hosting environment; their retention depends on that environment’s configuration.

  • Ended events: 90 days after an event ends, its signups, notices, pins, roster state and voice presence samples are deleted automatically. A small record of the event (its IDs and status) remains so it is never posted again.
  • Audit log entries are deleted 90 days after they are recorded.
  • Attendance records are kept after events end, so servers can see attendance history, until a deletion request or the server’s removal.
  • Servers that remove the bot: everything stored for that server is deleted 30 days after the removal, unless the bot is added back first.
  • Backups: the bot keeps copies of its database made before software upgrades; only the most recent three are kept. Other host backups follow the operator’s own practices.
  • Settings stay until the server removes the bot (see above) or asks the operator to delete them.

Withdrawing from an event is not a deletion request; see the next section. This policy does not promise that Discord’s own copies of messages are erased when the bot deletes a record.

6. Your choices and requests

Display redaction: run /privacy redact and confirm the private prompt within two minutes to replace your user mentions and displayed ID in the bot’s output with <redacted upon request>, across servers managed by this bot instance. Your signup, roster slot, roles and priority stay intact, and your records remain stored so matching, withdrawals and rejoining still work. This is not deletion: event timing and roles may still make you identifiable to other participants. /privacy resume undoes it for what the bot writes from then on; posts already rewritten stay redacted.

Deletion: run /privacy delete and confirm to delete your signups, roster places, pins, notes, warnings, date confirmations, role preferences and role fit, /whoami status, attendance records and voice presence samples in every server this instance serves, and to redact you in its older posts. The bot then stops tracking you: your Interested clicks are ignored, organizers cannot add you, and event descriptions skip you. Your Discord ID is kept on the redaction and do-not-track lists only so the request keeps being honored. Signing up yourself again (the Join button, /join) turns tracking back on; /privacy resume turns tracking back on and also shows your name again from then on. Deleted data isn’t restored.

Audit log and privacy requests: after /privacy delete, audit log entries about your own signups, server membership, and roles or nicknames the bot set at your request are deleted; after /privacy redact, they stop showing who you are. Entries for actions you took with a permission (for example, changing settings or editing rosters as an organizer) stay in that server’s audit log, because they are the server’s record of what was done, but your name is replaced at once by a request number such as <redacted upon request #0042>. So that a privacy request can’t be used to hide misuse of those permissions, the server’s admins can see which member made request #0042 for 72 hours; then the link between the number and you is deleted. A pattern of actions may still let people who were there recognize who took them.

The bot edits its own accessible historical messages in batches, with retries after failures. It cannot edit other people’s messages, Discord’s Interested list, screenshots, existing logs or backups, and it recognizes Discord mentions and IDs, not names typed by other people in custom text.

Do not send your password or bot tokens. We may ask for reasonable confirmation that the request concerns your account or a server you are authorized to manage. Requests will be handled promptly in accordance with applicable requirements. Depending on your location, you may also have rights to object to or restrict processing or complain to a data-protection authority.

7. Security and policy changes

Access to the bot host, database, and logs should be limited to those who need it to operate the service. No storage or transmission method is completely secure. The SQLite database is not encrypted by the application itself; storage protection depends on the hosting environment.

Changes to the bot’s data practices will be reflected here with an updated effective date. Material changes will be communicated through an available bot or support channel where required.
