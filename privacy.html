2. Information we process

The bot receives information through Discord’s API, event notifications, and slash commands. It can record people who mark Interested on a monitored event even if they never run a command. Authorized users can also add people to the bot’s signup records.

  • Identifiers: Discord user, server, event, role, channel, thread, and message IDs.
  • Signup and roster records: first-observed signup times, withdrawal and rejoin state, ordering, registered role IDs and names, role assignments, manual inclusion or exclusion, and, for recurring events, your Interested or Not interested answers for single dates and whether you confirmed, declined or didn't answer for each date (kept with attendance records).
  • Characters: members may save characters in a server: a name and a selection of roles they hold there. A character’s name is shown on rosters in that server and, for allied events, on the shared roster: to the hosting server and every other server the event is shared with. Attendance records also note the character’s name.
  • Event and member information: event names, descriptions, start times, status, creator identity, member names, and server roles used to display events, check permissions, and match players to slots. Some information is fetched as needed; event details can also appear in stored generated messages.
  • Configuration and delivery records: server and event settings, which release notes a server’s admins last read and who read them, templates, role groups, reminder requests and cooldowns, generated roster and notification text, mentioned user IDs, and message delivery or cleanup state.
  • Member roles (Server Members intent): when the operator enables it, Discord sends member and role updates so rosters can match players to slots and the bot can keep category roles in step. These are kept in memory, apart from recorded role assignments and the static memberships described below.
  • Attendance (servers on the Paid plan): during the event window, the bot samples presence every few minutes in any of the server’s voice or stage channels for roster members and the event’s creator only, even if the event has no voice channel. It keeps attendance marks with each event’s name, start time and final roster, first-seen voice times, and who confirmed attendance or voided the event. The creator’s first-seen voice time is used to account for a late leader.
  • Audit log (servers on the Paid plan): while a server is on the Paid plan, a record of who did what and when: settings changes, hand roster edits, attendance actions, each signup, withdrawal, standby move and recurring-date answer, members joining and leaving the server (with the Server Members intent), and the roles, nicknames, threads and posts the bot changed, with the member who caused it. Server admins (Manage Server) can read and download it.
  • Safety records: members a server’s admins blocked from giving themselves roles or signing up for events, and which elevated roles admins confirmed (with who confirmed them). Blocks are the server’s safety records, so a deletion request removes an admin’s name from them but doesn’t lift a block on the person asking. Security alerts (for example, a member pressing role buttons too fast) are posted to the admin channel a server chooses, which its admins can read. The bot counts recent presses in memory only, to pause members who press too fast.
  • Inbox records: operational problems and the permissions, roles or channels affected; security-alert text and any actor or subject IDs; release versions; and elevated-role permissions awaiting review. The bot records which admin read each item and revision, and who acknowledged an alert or approved or rejected a role review. Delivery records hold the destination channel, revision and delivery time, not a list of admins who received the post.
  • Restricted events: for events only some roles can join, the IDs of members kept on the roster because they signed up before the restriction applied, who opened enrollment for a date, and which members the bot added to a private event thread.
  • Shared events: when a server shares an event with an allied server, members of both servers who sign up are shown on the same roster in both, by display name and server, with the role they bring. The bot keeps which server each signup came from.
  • Applications: when you apply to play in another server’s event, the role (or roles) you offer, any note you add, your server, and whether you were accepted, until the event’s data is deleted.
  • Visitors: when you’re invited into another server as a Visitor for one event’s voice channel, the invite, and when you joined and were removed.
  • Alliances: which servers a server is allied with, the categories each shares, how each maps the other’s roles, and which admin made a code, formed or cancelled an alliance, or added another server’s shared event to their server’s calendar (alliance codes are kept only in a form that can’t be read back).
  • Statics: each static’s name, optional linked Discord role, leader, creator and member IDs. A static’s welcome message is written by its leader or admins, shown on its card and deleted with the static. It remains when its writer deletes their own data with /privacy, because it is the static’s content. Membership is stored in the database. For linked roles, the bot also records the membership last observed in Discord so manual role changes can update the group without overwriting database-only membership edits.
  • Bug reports: when you file one with /report-bug, what you write in the form, any files you attach, your Discord ID, the server, channel and event you filed it from, and a snapshot of diagnostics at that moment: the bot’s version, the server’s plan and settings, your roles and permissions in that server, and, from an event’s thread, the bot’s saved records about that event (which include other participants’ IDs and signups).
  • Subscriptions: Discord’s records of a server’s subscription to the bot (entitlement and product IDs and start and end times). Payments are handled by Discord; the bot never receives payment details.
  • Profile choices: the roles you say you prefer or would rather not play, the roles you’re never to be placed as in a given preset’s events (/whoami), and whether you have completed /whoami. Nickname changes are made in Discord. Role changes may also appear in the audit and static-membership records described above.
  • Privacy lists: the Discord IDs of people who asked for redaction or deletion, so those requests keep being honored.
  • Operational logs: connection and error information, signup actions, event and server IDs, and, for some actions, affected user IDs, role IDs, and the ID of the person making the change.

The bot reads relevant channel and thread history to recover its own previously sent messages and avoid duplicate posts. It does not maintain a general archive of members’ conversations. The bot does not request your Discord password, email address, payment details, or private direct-message history.

3. How information is used

Information is used to maintain signup order, apply role and capacity rules, manage withdrawal grace periods, display rosters, send reminders and schedule-change notices, record and summarize attendance where a server uses it, decide which features a server’s plan includes, enforce command permissions and cooldowns, recover after restarts, and troubleshoot failures. Attendance history may be used in the future to inform signup priority; this policy will be updated before that happens. The bot does not use this information for advertising, sell it, or use it to train AI models.

4. Who can see information

Signup names, mentions, selected roles, roster eligibility, and changes may appear in event threads and command responses. People who can access those Discord channels may see public messages. Some command responses are private to the invoking user, but underlying signup records are not necessarily private. Administrators and designated bot operators can manage records through authorized commands. Users authorized to manage bot settings can see Inbox records, including alert subjects and review outcomes; posts in the configured admin channel are visible to people with access to that channel.

Bug reports filed with /report-bug are posted in the bug channel of the Event Moogle community server, where its members can read them: what you wrote, your attachments, and a mention of you if you’re a member of that server (otherwise no name). The diagnostics posted with a report contain no names or IDs; the full snapshot stays in the database, for the operator and the bot’s developers.

The operator and hosting providers involved in running the service may access stored records and logs as needed to operate and support it. Data is sent to Discord to provide the bot’s features. Information may also be disclosed where required by law. Discord processes information under its own Privacy Policy.

5. Storage and retention

The bot stores records in a SQLite database on its host. In Docker deployments, the database normally lives in a persistent volume and survives container replacement and upgrades. Logs are written to the hosting environment; their retention depends on that environment’s configuration.

  • Ended events: 90 days after an event ends, its signups (including character links), notices, pins, roster state and voice presence samples are deleted automatically. Character names are cleared from its attendance records at the same time. A small record of the event (its IDs and status) remains so it is never posted again.
  • Audit log entries are deleted 90 days after they are recorded.
  • Inbox records: security alerts and release items expire 90 days after creation, even if unread. Active operational faults and pending elevated-role reviews remain until resolved; resolved items expire 90 days after resolution. Associated reads, reviews and delivery records are deleted with the item. Expiry does not acknowledge an alert or approve a role.
  • Characters: saved characters are kept while you remain in the server, unless you delete them or hide your name or delete your data with /privacy. Leaving the server deletes your saved characters there. Deleting a character returns linked signups to Myself; names in ended-event roster posts remain until normal retention or /privacy removes them. Deleting a server’s data also deletes its characters.
  • Attendance records are kept after events end, so servers can see attendance history, until a deletion request or the server’s removal.
  • Servers that remove the bot: everything stored for that server is deleted 30 days after the removal, unless the bot is added back first. Bug reports filed from it are kept, without the link to that server.
  • Bug reports and their diagnostics are deleted from the database 180 days after they are filed. The post in the community server is a message there and stays, apart from redaction (see the next section).
  • Backups: the bot keeps copies of its database made before software upgrades; only the most recent three are kept. Other host backups follow the operator’s own practices.
  • Settings stay until the server removes the bot (see above) or asks the operator to delete them.

Withdrawing from an event is not a deletion request; see the next section. This policy does not promise that Discord’s own copies of messages are erased when the bot deletes a record.

6. Your choices and requests

Display redaction: run /privacy → Redact identity and confirm the private prompt within two minutes to replace your user mentions and displayed ID in the bot’s output with <redacted upon request>, across servers managed by this bot instance. Your saved characters are deleted in every server this instance serves, linked signups return to Myself, and your character names are cleared from attendance records and hidden in the bot’s older roster posts. Your signups through Discord’s Interested, roster slots, roles and priority stay intact, and your signup records remain stored so matching, withdrawals and rejoining still work. The bot’s own sign-up buttons and everything across servers are then off for you: your signups for other servers’ events and pending applications are withdrawn, and you aren’t sent Visitor invites. This is not deletion: event timing and roles may still make you identifiable to other participants. /privacy → Resume undoes it for what the bot writes from then on; posts already rewritten stay redacted, and deleted characters are not restored.

Deletion: run /privacy → Delete my data and confirm to delete your characters, signups, roster places, pins, notes, warnings, date confirmations, role preferences and role fit, /whoami status, static memberships, Inbox read records, attendance records and voice presence samples in every server this instance serves, and to redact you in its older posts. Your identity is removed from static creator and leader records and Inbox review or security-alert actor and subject fields. Alert text is redacted for recognized mentions and IDs; event facts and review outcomes may remain under their retention rules. Your bug reports stop being linked to you and your ID is taken out of their diagnostics; what you wrote in them stays. The bot then stops tracking you: your Interested clicks are ignored, organizers cannot add you, and event descriptions skip you. Your Discord ID is kept on the redaction and do-not-track lists only so the request keeps being honored. Signing up yourself again (the Join button) turns tracking back on; /privacy → Resume turns tracking back on and also shows your name again from then on. Deleted data isn’t restored.

Audit log and privacy requests: after /privacy → Delete my data, audit log entries about your own signups, server membership, and roles or nicknames the bot set at your request are deleted; after /privacy → Redact identity, they stop showing who you are. Entries for actions you took with a permission (for example, changing settings or editing rosters as an organizer) stay in that server’s audit log, because they are the server’s record of what was done, but your name is replaced at once by a request number such as <redacted upon request #0042>. So that a privacy request can’t be used to hide misuse of those permissions, the server’s admins can see which member made request #0042 for 72 hours; then the link between the number and you is deleted. A pattern of actions may still let people who were there recognize who took them.

The bot edits its own accessible historical messages in batches, with retries after failures. It cannot edit other people’s messages, Discord’s Interested list, screenshots, existing logs or backups, and it recognizes Discord mentions and IDs, not names typed by other people in custom text.

To request access, correction or deletion outside Discord, contact the operator on Discord (user ID 642106888493858847). Include your Discord user ID and, if known, the relevant server or event. Do not send your password or bot tokens. We may ask for reasonable confirmation that the request concerns your account or a server you are authorized to manage. Requests will be handled promptly in accordance with applicable requirements. Depending on your location, you may also have rights to object to or restrict processing or complain to a data-protection authority.

7. Security and policy changes

Access to the bot host, database, and logs should be limited to those who need it to operate the service. No storage or transmission method is completely secure. The SQLite database is not encrypted by the application itself; storage protection depends on the hosting environment.

Changes to the bot’s data practices will be reflected here with an updated effective date. Material changes will be communicated through an available bot or support channel where required.
